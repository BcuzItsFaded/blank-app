# La Mataviejitas — factual notes
Episode 26 of **PLOT TWIST WITH A&R** (paper-cut lightbox, ~59s, English). Condensed from the
three-case script and fact-checked against Wikipedia ("Juana Barraza").

## Documented
- **Juana Barraza Samperio** (born 27 Dec 1957) killed elderly women in **Mexico City** between
  about **1998 and 2006**. The press called the killer **La Mataviejitas**.
- For years police suspected a man, possibly disguised as a woman.
- She was a professional wrestler known as **"La Dama del Silencio"** (the Lady of Silence).
- **25 January 2006:** she was arrested fleeing the home of **Ana María de los Reyes Alfaro, 82**.
- Convicted of **16 murders** and sentenced to **759 years**. Authorities and the press linked her to
  **as many as 40 or more** deaths.
- Investigators **suspected** she posed as a government or social worker to get inside.

## Corrected from the source script
- "Two other suspects were publicly arrested in 2005": not in the source, so it was dropped.
- "A tenant saw her and called a patrol": not in the source, so the film says only "caught fleeing".
- "Posed as a nurse": the source gives a suspected government or welfare official, so the film says
  "investigators believed… a social worker".
- The weapon and the victim's name are left out of the narration.

## Long version (~2:05): added detail
- Span: about 1998–2006. By 2005, the media were criticising officials for calling serial-killer reports
  "media sensationalism".
- Method (suspected): posing as a government worker offering welfare programs or pensions.
- Witnesses described a "masculine-looking woman". Police hunted a man in disguise and swept through Mexico
  City's transgender sex-worker community (described by journalists as "ham-fisted").
- Red herring: at least three victims owned a print of Greuze's *Boy in a Red Waistcoat*. The film shows a
  stylised red-waistcoat figure, not the painting.
- Barraza was born in 1957 in Epazoyucan, Hidalgo. Her mother is described as an alcoholic. Further abuse
  described in sources is deliberately not narrated.
- Fingerprints linked her to at least ten of about forty murders. She admitted to four (Alfaro and three others),
  and said her motive was resentment toward her mother.
- Convicted 31 March 2008 on sixteen charges. Sentence: 759 years. After her arrest, officials closed the
  investigation "despite more than 30 unresolved cases".
