# THE DANCING PLAGUE — treatment
PLOT TWIST WITH A&R · Episode 19 · vertical 9:16 · ~62s · paper-cut lightbox · English

**Look.** Layered cut paper, backlit by a hot July dusk over Strasbourg, with the
cathedral's single spire and the rose window lit from behind. Half-timbered rooftops
have glowing cut-out windows. The dancers are animated black silhouettes whose skirts
and arms whip in time. The one accent colour is **red shoes**, glowing on every
dancer, then left alone on the cobbles at the end.

1. **0:00 Hook.** The whole street dances against the sunset. Title cards.
2. **0:10 The first woman.** Frau Troffea dances alone in a narrow lane while two
   onlookers stand frozen.
3. **0:17 It spreads.** Two rows of dancers. At 0:20 they tire and slump while
   faces watch from the lit windows.
4. **0:24 The doctors.** A physician holds his flask up to a blazing sun. Night
   falls and a zodiac wheel of stars turns overhead.
5. **0:32 The city's response.** The council's edict drops onto the hall:
   NO DANCING, NO DRUMS, with a red seal. The drum is struck out in red.
6. **0:39 Until death?** A pale paper skeleton dances with a woman under a blood
   moon. She collapses. Cards: HUNDREDS AFFECTED? · DEATHS · DISPUTED.
7. **0:47 Saint Vitus.** Sufferers climb a hill towards his glowing shrine.
8. **0:54 The twist.** One dancer under a full moon slows and fades away. Only her
   glowing red shoes remain. THE CAUSE REMAINS UNKNOWN.

**Sound.** British storyteller narration. The score is a pipe-and-tabor jig
(recorder, shawm-like oboe, frame drum), because the city really hired musicians.
It speeds up, frays as the dancers tire, and goes silent when the bass drum
lands on the ban. It returns as a slow organ dirge for the danse macabre, then
church bells and chimes at the shrine. Finally the jig plays once more, slowing
until it stops unresolved. Foley: stamping feet locked to the beat, crowd murmur,
July crickets, the edict nailed up, rattling bones, a fall, pilgrims' steps.
Mastered to −14 LUFS.
