In 1999, a gentle giant on death row made millions of people cry. The Green Mile is one of Stephen King's most heartbreaking stories, and its ending hides a curse that a lot of people miss. Full spoilers ahead. If you haven't seen it, stop right here.

The Green Mile was directed by Frank Darabont, based on Stephen King's 1996 novel. It opens in a nursing home, where an old man named Paul Edgecomb breaks down crying while watching an old Fred Astaire movie, Top Hat. He tells his friend Elaine why, and the story takes us back to 1935.

Paul, played by Tom Hanks, runs death row at Cold Mountain Penitentiary. The prisoners call it the Green Mile, because of its lime-green floor. His team includes Brutus "Brutal" Howell, played by David Morse, and the cruel, cowardly Percy Wetmore, who keeps his job because he's related to the governor's wife.

Then John Coffey arrives. He's enormous, played by Michael Clarke Duncan, and he's been convicted of murdering two little girls, the Detterick twins. But Coffey is gentle, and afraid of the dark. One day, he grabs Paul through the bars and cures his painful bladder infection. Then he breathes out a cloud of tiny black insects. That's how Coffey heals: he takes the sickness into himself, and lets it out.

Another prisoner, Eduard Delacroix, has a pet mouse named Mr. Jingles. Out of pure spite, Percy stomps on the mouse. Coffey brings it back to life. But Percy gets his revenge anyway. At Del's execution, he leaves the sponge dry on purpose, and Del dies horribly in the electric chair.

Meanwhile, the warden's wife, Melinda, is dying of a brain tumor. Paul and his men drug a violent new inmate, "Wild Bill" Wharton, lock Percy in a straitjacket, and sneak Coffey out of the prison at night. Coffey heals Melinda. But this time, he doesn't breathe the sickness out.

Back on the Mile, Coffey grabs Percy and breathes it into him. In a trance, Percy shoots Wild Bill dead, then goes catatonic, and ends up a patient in an asylum. Why Wild Bill? Coffey takes Paul's hand and shows him what he saw: Wild Bill Wharton killed the twins. Coffey found the girls and tried to save them. He was innocent all along.

Paul offers to let him escape. Coffey refuses. He says he's tired of feeling all the pain in the world, every single day. His last wish is to watch a movie, and they show him Top Hat. "They's angels," he whispers. At his execution, Coffey asks not to wear the hood, because he's afraid of the dark. Paul shakes his hand. And the guards, in tears, carry it out.

Back in the present, Paul reveals his age: one hundred and eight. When Coffey took his hand, he passed a piece of his power into Paul. Mr. Jingles got some too: the little mouse is still alive, decades later. Paul has outlived his wife, and everyone he ever loved. He believes it's his punishment for letting a miracle of God die.

His final words: "We each owe a death. There are no exceptions. But, oh God, sometimes the Green Mile seems so long." John Coffey's initials are J.C., and many fans see him as a Christ figure: an innocent man who takes on the world's pain, and dies for it. Did this ending wreck you too?
