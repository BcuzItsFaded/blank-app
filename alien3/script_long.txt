Remember how Aliens ended? Ripley defeated the Alien Queen, rescued Newt, and escaped with Hicks and Bishop. For the first time, it seemed she might actually have a family again. But Alien 3 destroys that hope almost immediately. While everyone is in hypersleep aboard the Sulaco, an electrical fire triggers the emergency escape system. Their escape vehicle is launched into space and crashes onto a remote prison planet called Fiorina 161. Ripley survives, but Hicks and Newt are killed, and Bishop is left in pieces. Everything she fought to save is suddenly gone.

Ripley isn't just grieving. She's already investigating. Clemens, the prison doctor, tells her Newt drowned when the escape vehicle crashed, while Hicks suffered fatal injuries. But Ripley notices suspicious damage near Newt's cryotube. She's seen what an alien parasite can do, and she fears something came aboard the Sulaco before the accident. That's why she demands an autopsy. No embryo is found inside Newt, but Ripley still insists on cremating both bodies. She doesn't explain the entire threat to the prison authorities. She simply refuses to take another chance.

Fiorina 161 isn't an ordinary prison. It's an aging industrial foundry operated for Weyland-Yutani, with a small group of convicted criminals who chose to remain after the larger facility closed. These men have developed a strict religious code, including vows of celibacy, under a powerful prisoner named Dillon. Ripley's arrival threatens their isolated routine. Even more dangerous, the facility has no conventional firearms. Unlike the Colonial Marines, these prisoners cannot simply shoot an alien. And although Dillon distrusts Ripley at first, his beliefs will become essential when the survivors must decide whether to fight or surrender.

While the prison conducts its funeral, another nightmare is beginning. A facehugger has infected a dog near the wreckage. The resulting alien isn't built like the towering creature aboard the Nostromo or the warriors on LV-426. This one moves on four legs, runs at incredible speed, and navigates the prison's tight industrial passages with terrifying ease. Its first attacks are difficult to explain because the witnesses barely understand what they're seeing. One maintenance worker, Murphy, encounters the creature and dies near a giant ventilation fan. And the prison still has no idea what is hunting them.

Ripley searches the prison scrapyard for Bishop's damaged remains, hoping the android can explain what happened aboard the Sulaco. But several prisoners corner her, and Dillon is forced to intervene. After recovering Bishop, Ripley reconnects him to the escape vehicle's flight recorder. His findings confirm her worst fear. An alien was aboard, and it traveled with them all the way to Fiorina 161. Even more disturbing, the company has already received the ship's information. Weyland-Yutani knows what's happening, and Bishop believes they want the creature. Ripley is beginning to understand why rescue is suddenly so important.

The alien's attacks are becoming impossible to dismiss. Two prisoners are slaughtered inside the maintenance tunnels, leaving a traumatized witness named Golic. But Superintendent Andrews still refuses to believe a monster is responsible. Then the creature enters the infirmary and kills Doctor Clemens, one of the few people Ripley has begun to trust. What happens next is even stranger. The alien approaches Ripley, brings its jaws inches from her face, and simply leaves. She has no explanation. Moments later, she warns the prisoners, only for Andrews himself to be dragged into the ceiling.

With Andrews dead, the survivors finally accept that Ripley was telling the truth. But accepting the danger doesn't mean they can defeat it. Fiorina 161 has no pulse rifles, flamethrowers, or military equipment. Their only advantage is the industrial structure itself. Ripley proposes using flammable chemicals to drive the creature through the ventilation system and into a containment chamber. The prisoners begin preparing the trap, but their improvised strategy turns disastrous. During an attack, a dropped flare ignites the chemicals. Fire tears through the connected passages, killing many prisoners and leaving the survivors with even fewer options.

Ripley begins feeling increasingly ill, and a medical scan reveals the most horrifying discovery in the entire movie. An Alien Queen is growing inside her body. Suddenly, the creature's earlier behavior makes sense. It wasn't showing mercy when it refused to kill Ripley. It was preserving the host carrying its future Queen. But this discovery creates another terrifying possibility. If Weyland-Yutani removes the embryo and keeps it alive, the company won't just possess one dangerous creature. It could potentially breed countless more. Ripley realizes that saving herself might be the worst possible outcome for everyone else.

Weyland-Yutani is now sending a recovery team, but Ripley understands what the company really wants. For years, its representatives have been willing to sacrifice human lives to obtain a living alien. Now a Queen is growing inside the one person who understands the danger. Ripley decides that the embryo must die, even if she has to die with it. She approaches Dillon and asks him to kill her. But Dillon refuses to let her leave the other prisoners helpless. He'll help her end her suffering, only after they destroy the creature already hunting his people.

Dillon gathers the surviving prisoners and presents one last possibility. They can't overpower the alien, but they might be able to outmaneuver it. The foundry contains a maze of corridors with heavy doors that can be closed behind a moving target. By using themselves as bait, the prisoners hope to guide the creature toward a chamber where molten lead can be released. It's a desperate strategy because every runner must survive long enough to close the correct door. One mistake could trap another prisoner with the alien. But waiting for the company offers no trustworthy escape either.

The final chase becomes a massacre. Doors jam, prisoners lose their routes, and the alien kills nearly everyone who tries to guide it. Somehow, Ripley, Dillon, and Morse get the creature into the foundry's lead-mold chamber. But the alien still needs to be kept in place. Dillon makes the ultimate sacrifice, remaining inside while Morse releases molten lead onto the monster. Even that isn't enough. The creature erupts from the molten metal and pursues Ripley. She activates the water system, rapidly cooling its overheated body. The resulting thermal shock finally destroys the adult Xenomorph.

The adult alien is dead, but Ripley's nightmare isn't finished. Weyland-Yutani's recovery team arrives with a man who looks exactly like Bishop. He claims he can remove the Queen and save her life. Ripley knows the company's history, and she refuses to gamble humanity's future on another promise. As the confrontation turns violent, she reaches the platform above the furnace. Then she makes her final choice. Ripley falls into the molten metal, taking the emerging Queen with her. Morse survives, the prison closes, and Ripley's original journey ends with the one sacrifice the company cannot undo.
