# The Ninth House: treatment

Style: Watercolor Brush. Length about 60 s. English, calm male narrator, burned-in subtitles + .srt. 1920x1080, 24 fps.

## 1. Three candidate structures

A. **A journal kept over one week.** One locked page per day; each page is painted over the last, and each morning the page contains one thing the painter did not paint. Escalation by days, the horror arrives through the journal itself.
B. **A single long handscroll walk.** The camera travels the lane once, left to right, and the lane changes as we pass (houses appear that were not there). Continuous, but the turn has no "evidence" beat and the ending is only a camera arrival.
C. **Backwards painting.** Open on a finished painting that un-paints itself to the first stroke, and reveal the painter is also painted. Strong ending, weak middle, hard to give a person to care about.

**Chosen: A, with C's idea as the last beat.** The week gives the Twilight Zone shape (ordinary rule, small impossible deviation, rationalisation, escalation, reveal) and suits a watercolor "field journal" where pages are the native unit. The reveal borrows from C: the lane washes back to paper and the painter turns out to be painted.

Opening: not the lane but a hand-written line on blank paper (the rule). Ending: the page left unfinished with the brush lifted mid-stroke, then a fresh sheet slides in and begins again. Score: sparse, one idea that gets quietly wrong.

## 2. Logline and arc

A woman who has painted the same eight-house lane every morning for eleven years finds a ninth house in her journal, and learns who has been holding the brush.

Setup (0-14 s): the lane, the rule: she paints only what she sees.
Turn (14-34 s): a ninth house with a red door appears in her hand; the lane has eight; next day it has nine and everyone says it was always there.
Escalation (34-48 s): each page adds a light, a table, a woman painting. On Saturday the woman looks up.
Ending (48-62 s): Marta crosses the lane, her own house washes to paper, the page is left unfinished, a new sheet begins: "Entry 1".

## 3. Benchmark

Learn: the pacing of a short anthology episode (one rule, one crack, one reveal, no explanation after), and the naturalist field-journal page (specimen + note, dated entries) for composition. Don't take: any existing episode, twist, character, narrator wording or music; no real artwork.

## 6. Cue map

Tempo 66 bpm (beat = 0.909 s), 4/4, A minor leaning to Dorian. Sections: Intro 0-14 (kalimba + soft pad, melody in kalimba, 8 bars of one-note ostinato E-A); Crack 14-33 (add felt piano with one wrong note, a flat 2nd, on bar boundaries; glockenspiel on every page turn); Escalation 33-44 (strings low, ostinato slows); Silence 44-46.5 (all music out, paper rustle only); Look 46.5-55 (pad only, no rhythm); Close 55-62 (kalimba ostinato returns alone, last note not resolved). Cuts and page turns land on bar lines; the reveal look on a downbeat.

## 8. Subtitles and titles

Serif italic (Cormorant Garamond) ~36 px at the bottom on paper halo, English only, held at least 1.8 s. Hand notes in Caveat (dates, "No. 9?"), mono labels in IBM Plex Mono. Final entry written in a different hand (Reenie Beanie) to mark the new painter.

## Final timing note
The delivered film runs 73.5 s (narration paced for silences at 47-49.5 s and 58.6-61 s); days run Tuesday to Monday, and the look-up lands on Monday.
