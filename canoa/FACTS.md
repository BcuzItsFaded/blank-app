# San Miguel Canoa, 1968 — factual notes

Episode 23 of **PLOT TWIST WITH A&R** (paper-cut lightbox, ~61s, English).
**Documented history, not a ghost story.**

## Documented (both main sources agree)
- **14 September 1968**, San Miguel Canoa, Puebla, Mexico.
- **Five employees of the Universidad Autónoma de Puebla** were headed to climb the volcano
  **La Malinche**. Rain and nightfall made them stop in the village for the night.
- A local man, **Lucas García**, gave them shelter in his home.
- The parish priest, **Enrique Meza Pérez**, had stirred anti-communist fear. The church
  bells rang that night, and the men were accused of being communists.
- A mob attacked the house. **Four people were killed**: university workers **Jesús Carrillo
  Sánchez** and **Ramón Gutiérrez Calvario**, the host **Lucas García**, and a relative of his.
  The **three other workers survived with grave injuries** (Julián González Báez, Roberto
  Rojano Aguirre, Miguel Flores Cruz).
- The priest was **never convicted**.

## Per Mexico's National Archives (AGN) only
- About **800 people** took part. The shouts included "¡Ya llegaron los comunistas!",
  "¡Vienen a matarnos!" and "¡Se van a robar a las mujeres!"
- The fourth victim was **Pedro García**, Lucas's brother. Wikipedia does not name him, so the
  narration says only "four people died".

## Where the sources disagree (handled carefully)
- **Punishment:** the AGN says there were no arrests or punishment at all. Wikipedia, citing
  the closing text of Felipe Cazals's 1976 film *Canoa: A Shameful Memory*, reports a few
  prosecutions of townspeople. The film only states what both sources agree on: **the priest
  was never convicted**.
- **First contact:** the AGN says Pedro García first met the men in a shop. Wikipedia names
  Odilón García.

## Depiction
- No violence, blood or bodies are shown. Villagers are faceless silhouettes with torches.
  The deaths are shown as four small flames going out on a windowsill, with the victims'
  names. The survivors are mentioned only in narration.
- "There was no ghost in Canoa" is the series' framing. The horror is human.
