# Facts and sources

La Planchada is a traditional Mexican hospital ghost legend. Nothing in this film is a documented case. The narration is the author's script, kept word for word; every story beat is framed as legend ("according to legend", "some versions say", "patients have claimed", "supposedly").

| Claim in the film | Status | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Patients in Mexican hospitals tell of a ghostly night nurse | Folklore, widely told | Cultura Genial, "La planchada: la leyenda de la enfermera fantasma" (https://www.culturagenial.com/es/leyenda-de-la-planchada/); Chilango, "Eulalia, el fantasma chilango de La Planchada" (https://www.chilango.com/cultura/leyenda-de-la-planchada/) |
| Old-fashioned white uniform, perfectly ironed (the nickname "La Planchada" = "the ironed one") | Folklore; the nickname is explained this way across versions | same |
| She loved a doctor who betrayed her | One version among several (an unrequited love is the most common) | Excélsior, "Leyenda entre el desamor y la traición... 'La Planchada'" (https://www.excelsior.com.mx/nacional/2017/10/27/1125955) |
| She died heartbroken, or neglected her patients and cannot rest | Two competing versions, both presented as such | same, and Cultura Genial above |
| She comforts patients, checks their medicine; nobody at the hospital knows the nurse they describe; she died years earlier | Folklore; a "patients later discover" motif | Cultura Genial / TV Azteca, "Leyenda de La Planchada" (https://www.tvazteca.com/amastv/especiales-a/leyenda-planchada-escalofriante-historia-hospital) |
| "She returns because she never stopped caring for her patients" | The benevolent reading of the legend (the film's chosen ending) | Cultura Genial above |

The name "Eulalia", the Hospital Juárez setting and any dates vary between sources and are not used in the film. Ending screen: "The nurse is a legend. Her story still haunts Mexico."
