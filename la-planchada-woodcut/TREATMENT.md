# La Planchada (woodcut): treatment

Style: Woodcut Print. 9:16 vertical, 24 fps, 62.4 s (the last 3.4 s hold the ending plate), English narration, letterpress captions in the paper margin.

**Structure chosen:** a wordless-novel sequence of 22 self-contained plates, each carved stroke by stroke in front of us and each hard-cut as a new impression (a two-frame misregistration). Considered: one long continuous carve that grows into a world (too slow for eight beats) and a single mirrored-block reveal (too little story). Chosen because a woodcut novel is built for a legend told in short, graphic pages.

**Logline:** a night nurse who loves her patients too much to leave them.
**Arc:** the title block is pulled and the print appears → a corridor carved from one light at the end → the pressed uniform in pleats of white and black → a memory under a sun-arch; the **one colour plate** (oxblood) appears only for hearts, split by the betrayal → two versions of her death (rain and a rising spirit; a bell and a spirit pulled back like a rubber band) → the night visit; a hand on a brow → morning rays, three blank faces and three question marks → a wall of photographs; a snap zoom on her portrait; a flight → she tucks in a patient; a heart carved in colour rises, then withdraws to a single highlight → the closing plate with carved letters.
**Native moves:** light is carved (radial cuts from a lamp, a moon, a window); carving = time; the one colour plate spreads and withdraws; wordless-novel sequencing; the paper transition (the sheet peels away) at the change from day to night.
**Benchmark (grammar only):** wordless woodcut novels (Masereel, Lynd Ward) for sequencing; Kollwitz for how emotion sits in the direction of the cut. No existing plate is copied.
**Sound:** low strings and wood and skin: contrabass pizzicato, cellos, frame drum, woodblock, gran cassa, one tam-tam; foley is knife into wood (a rasp tied to each plate's carving), a paper slap on every cut; hard silences at 21.0-21.5 s and 46.7-49.1 s.
**Rendering note:** a Canvas 2D engine written for this film: a tone picture and an id map are turned into form-following cuts by tracing streamlines along per-shape direction fields, with cut width following tone; ink grain, ragged block edge and a small colour misregistration are added in the print pass.
