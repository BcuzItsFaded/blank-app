Ever feel like you're standing in your own way? Gary John Bishop's Unfuck Yourself has a rude awakening: thinking about changing your life isn't the same as actually changing it.
Lesson one: are you willing? Not just willing to dream about a better life, but willing to do the uncomfortable work required to build one. There's a difference.
That could mean finally applying for a job, having a difficult conversation, or starting something you've postponed for months. The point isn't to feel ready. It's to begin.
Lesson two: you're wired to win, but maybe at the wrong game. Bishop argues that we can become surprisingly good at repeating the very behaviors that keep us stuck.
If you're constantly avoiding rejection, you've become good at avoiding rejection. But what if you redirected that same determination toward practicing, learning, and actually trying?
Lesson three: embrace uncertainty. Most people want a guarantee before they take a risk. But life rarely tells you exactly how things will work out.
Waiting for fear to disappear can keep you waiting forever. You don't need to know every step of the journey. Sometimes you just need the courage to take the next one.
Lesson four: you're not your thoughts. You're what you do. Your mind might tell you you're not good enough, not talented enough, or not ready. But a thought isn't an order.
Confidence doesn't always come before action. Sometimes it grows afterward. You write one page, make one attempt, or show up one more day. And suddenly, you have proof that you can move forward.
Lesson five: expect nothing and accept everything. That doesn't mean giving up or accepting mistreatment. It means facing reality instead of wasting your energy demanding that everything happen exactly as planned.
Maybe your first attempt fails. Maybe the opportunity disappears. You can learn, adjust, and try again. That's what being relentless can look like: not never struggling, but refusing to stop making meaningful choices.
