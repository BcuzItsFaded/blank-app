Four books. Four movies. And one of the biggest fake-outs in movie history. Today we're breaking down every Twilight book: the plot, the biggest twist, the ending, and how each one changed on the big screen. Full spoilers for the whole saga ahead.

Book one: Twilight, from 2005. Seventeen-year-old Bella Swan moves from sunny Phoenix to rainy Forks, Washington, to live with her dad, Charlie. At school she meets Edward Cullen, who acts like he can't stand her. Then he stops a skidding van with his bare hands to save her life. Bella pieces it together: Edward is a vampire. His family, the Cullens, only drink animal blood. And Bella's scent is the most tempting thing he has ever smelled.

The twist comes at a baseball game in a thunderstorm. Three nomad vampires show up: Laurent, Victoria, and James, a tracker who treats Bella like a game. The Cullens hide her in Phoenix, but James calls her with her mother's voice, and lures her to her old ballet studio. Her mom was never there. The voice came from an old home video.

James attacks and bites Bella's wrist. Edward and his family arrive just in time and destroy James. Then Edward has to suck the venom out of her blood, and somehow, he stops. The book ends at prom, with Bella begging Edward to make her a vampire. He refuses.

The 2008 film, starring Kristen Stewart and Robert Pattinson, keeps the story, but moves things around. The famous "Say it. Out loud." moment happens in the woods, but in the book, Bella confronts him in his car. The movie shows the nomads hunting people near Forks from the start. And the final shot is film-only: Victoria, watching the prom from above, planning her revenge.

Book two: New Moon. On Bella's eighteenth birthday, she gets a paper cut at the Cullens' house, and Jasper lunges at her. Terrified of what could happen, Edward leaves Forks with his whole family, and tells Bella he doesn't want her anymore. She falls apart for months. Slowly, her best friend Jacob Black brings her back to life, until Jacob starts disappearing too.

The twist: Jacob is a werewolf. The Quileute legends are true, and his pack protects the town from vampires, including Victoria, who is now hunting Bella to avenge James. Meanwhile, Bella discovers that when she does something reckless, she hears Edward's voice. So she goes cliff diving.

Alice sees a vision of Bella jumping, and word reaches Edward that she's dead. Heartbroken, he goes to the Volturi, the vampire royalty in Italy, to make them kill him. Bella races to Volterra and stops him seconds before he steps into the sunlight. Edward admits he lied: he left to protect her, and never stopped loving her. The Volturi let them go, on one condition. Bella must become a vampire.

The 2009 film follows the book closely, but it had to show months of heartbreak. The book uses almost blank pages, with just the names of the months. The film spins the camera around Bella at her window as the seasons change. In the movie, Bella doesn't just hear Edward, she sees him. And it ends on a cliffhanger: Edward will change her himself, on one condition. Marry me.

Book three: Eclipse. A wave of mysterious killings hits Seattle. The cause: newborn vampires, wild, strong, and thirsty. At the same time, Bella is caught between Edward and Jacob, who both love her, and can barely stand each other. When the danger closes in, the Cullens and the wolf pack do the unthinkable. They team up.

The twist: Victoria built that army. She turned a young man named Riley, made him believe she loved him, and sent him to create newborns, all with one target. Bella. And there's a second twist. The Volturi knew about the army, and waited, hoping it would wipe out the Cullens for them.

The vampires and wolves crush the newborn army. On the mountain, Edward destroys Victoria, while young Seth Clearwater takes down Riley. When the Volturi finally arrive, they execute Bree, a newborn who had surrendered. And in the end, Bella makes her choice. She says yes to marrying Edward, and a heartbroken Jacob runs away in wolf form.

The 2010 film has one big advantage. The book is told only through Bella's eyes, so we just hear about the killings. The movie opens in Seattle, with Riley being hunted by Victoria, and lets us watch the army grow. It also cuts the sleepover where Rosalie tells her tragic story, and it adds a new speech in the meadow, where Bella says she isn't choosing between Edward and Jacob. She's choosing who she really is.

Book four: Breaking Dawn, the biggest of them all. Bella and Edward get married, and honeymoon on a private island. Then, the impossible: Bella is pregnant. The half-vampire baby grows at terrifying speed, breaking her bones from the inside. And for a whole section of the book, the story switches to Jacob's point of view, as he breaks from his pack to protect her.

The birth nearly kills Bella. To save her, Edward injects his venom straight into her heart, and Bella finally becomes a vampire. Then comes the twist fans still argue about. Jacob imprints on the baby, Renesmee, a wolf bond that means he'll be whatever she needs, forever. And Bella discovers her own power: a shield that protects minds.

A vampire named Irina spots Renesmee and mistakes her for an immortal child, which is forbidden. The Volturi march on Forks. The Cullens gather witnesses from around the world, and Alice and Jasper seem to abandon them, but they're really searching for proof: another half-vampire, named Nahuel. In a snowy field, Bella's shield protects everyone, Alice returns with Nahuel, and the Volturi simply leave. No battle at all.

And that's the biggest change in the whole saga. Breaking Dawn was split into two films, in 2011 and 2012. Part Two gives fans the battle the book never had. Carlisle loses his head. Jasper, Seth, and Leah fall. And Bella and Edward finally take down Aro. Then, the reveal: it was all a vision that Alice showed Aro. None of it happened. Aro sees he would lose, and the Volturi walk away, just like in the book.

In the end, Bella lets Edward into her mind for the first time, and they look ahead to forever. And if you want even more, there's Midnight Sun, from 2020, which retells the first book from Edward's point of view.
