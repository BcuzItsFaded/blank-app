In 1947, Godzilla destroyed Ginza, and a former kamikaze pilot finally stopped running. He beat the monster, found the woman he thought was dead, and sank Godzilla to the bottom of the sea. Happy ending, right? Wrong. Because Godzilla never really dies. And this time, the danger isn't just coming from the ocean. It's coming from the sky, and from inside his own home.
This is Godzilla Minus Zero, explained.
First, let's go back. In Godzilla Minus One, it's 1945, and Koichi Shikishima is a kamikaze pilot who can't make himself die. He fakes engine trouble and lands on Odo Island. That night, Godzilla attacks the base. Koichi freezes in the cockpit and never fires. Almost everyone dies, and the mechanic Tachibana never forgives him.
Koichi returns to a burned-out Tokyo. His parents are dead. But he meets Noriko, a young woman carrying an orphaned baby named Akiko, and the three of them become a family. Except Koichi won't call it one. He doesn't think he deserves to live.
Then Godzilla comes back, bigger, after American nuclear tests at Bikini Atoll. It crushes Ginza, where Noriko works. She shoves Koichi to safety, and the blast wave takes her.
So Koichi joins a plan led by an engineer named Kenji Noda: sink Godzilla in Sagami Bay with Freon tanks, then drag it up so fast the pressure tears it apart. Koichi plans to finish the job by flying a fighter plane straight into Godzilla's mouth. A suicide mission.
It works. Godzilla's head explodes. But Koichi lives, because Tachibana secretly built him an ejection seat. And then comes the miracle. Noriko is alive, in a hospital. But there's a dark mark spreading on her neck. And at the bottom of the bay, Godzilla's flesh starts to grow back.
Now, Minus Zero. It's 1949, two years later. Japan is still rebuilding. Koichi and Noriko are married, and they're raising Akiko with help from their neighbor Sumiko, who now runs an orphanage. For the first time, Koichi has a real life.
But Noriko is changing. Godzilla's cells, the G-cells, are inside her body, and they're mutating her. She survived Godzilla. Now part of Godzilla is surviving inside her.
Meanwhile, Noda hasn't stopped working either. He now runs the Large-Scale Disaster Response Bureau, and he pulls the old crew back together: the young Mizushima, Captain Akitsu, and Hotta.
And they're going to need them. Because something has just landed near the city of Matsumoto. A meteorite. And inside it is a gelatinous organism that starts eating the local wildlife. The more it eats, the more it changes. Until it becomes a dragon that can fly, and fire electric beams.
Two scientists start studying it. Kanji Murakami, who survived the atomic bombing of Hiroshima, and Akira Hagiwara. They give the creature a name: Ghidora.
A meteorologist named Makoto Aoyama notices a pattern. Ghidora keeps showing up where lightning storms are. It isn't just using electricity. It's drawn to it.
While Ghidora lies dormant, Koichi volunteers for another mission. The Americans agree to help, reluctantly. A bomber carries a brand-new fuel-air bomb, escorted by Skyraider fighters.
The bomb hits it dead-on. For a moment, it looks like it worked. Then Ghidora rises out of the fire, and wipes out the escort pilots. Koichi survives. Again.
So the bureau tries a smarter idea. If the monster loves lightning, give it too much. They fire harpoons tipped with Narukami rods into Ghidora's body, and wait for the storm.
The lightning strikes. Again and again. Ghidora collapses, and this time, it seems dead.
It isn't. The lightning didn't kill it. It fed it. Ghidora comes back bigger, with three heads, two tails, and a new power: a gravity beam that lifts whole buildings into the air and smashes them down. Ghidora has become King Ghidora.
And then, off the island of Kozu-shima, the sea starts to boil. The thing everyone feared is back. Godzilla has fully regenerated.
Now Japan has two monsters. And Murakami has an idea. What if they fight each other?
But Murakami has a secret. He lost his family at Hiroshima. And he wants revenge. His real plan is to steer Godzilla across the ocean, to destroy New York City.
To control Godzilla, he's built a machine: the Resonance Wave Amplifier. But it only works with one thing. Godzilla's own cells. Which means it only works with Noriko.
When Koichi finds out, he does what he always does. He runs. He grabs Noriko and Akiko and tries to get them as far away as possible.
But in the ruins of Matsumoto, Noriko sees what Ghidora has done. And she makes her own choice. She volunteers. If her body can stop this, she wants it used.
Noda confronts Murakami. He doesn't fight him. He talks him down. And Murakami gives up his revenge.
The new plan. The bureau turns the ruins of Ginza into a battlefield, packed with generators and traps. The same place where Godzilla nearly killed Noriko two years ago.
Koichi takes off in Murakami's old flying boat, a Kawanishi H8K, with Noriko and the amplifier on board. They fly low over the sea, and use her signal to pull Godzilla toward Tokyo.
It works. Godzilla wades ashore, and walks straight into King Ghidora.
The fight is brutal. Godzilla grabs one of Ghidora's heads and tears it clean off. But Ghidora bites off Godzilla's arm. Then its gravity beam pulls down the buildings around him, and buries Godzilla under the rubble.
Now the humans have to finish it. Trucks spray liquid nitrogen until Ghidora is frozen solid. Then they soak it in fuel. The plan is temperature shock: freeze it, then burn it.
But the fuel lines are cut. So Noda drives in to set the trap off by hand. Ghidora's beam wrecks his car.
Koichi fires a rocket. The fuel ignites, and the explosion engulfs Ghidora, and Noda with it. Koichi has just killed his friend.
Aoyama sees his face. And she lies to him. She tells him Ghidora killed Noda. It's a kind lie, so he can keep going.
And Koichi has to keep going. Because Ghidora still isn't dead. Koichi and Noriko switch on the amplifier and wake Godzilla up under the rubble.
But the severed head has absorbed Godzilla's cells. When Ghidora reattaches it, that head grows spines like Godzilla's, and it has a heat ray of its own.
Godzilla rises. Beam against beam, the two monsters lock in place. But Ghidora can't control a power it was never born with. Its stolen heat ray goes wild, and Godzilla finishes it.
So what does the ending mean? American forces open fire on Godzilla. Then they're ordered to stop, and keep him alive, because now he's a potential weapon. Godzilla returns to the sea.
Murakami burns his notes and the flying boat, and tries to escape with Hagiwara. American soldiers arrest them both for possessing classified information.
And Koichi realizes the truth. If the military wants Godzilla as a weapon, they'll want the only person whose body can control him. They'll want Noriko.
So Koichi and Noriko decide to disappear. At first, they plan to leave Akiko behind, where she'd be safe. But they can't do it. They take her with them, and the family goes into hiding together.
Here's one way to read it. Minus One was about a man who refused to live. Minus Zero is about a family that refuses to be used. The war is over, but they still aren't free.
Behind the scenes, director Takashi Yamazaki had a five-page outline for this sequel before Minus One even came out. Minus One won the Academy Award for Best Visual Effects on a budget reported at about ten million dollars. For Minus Zero, Yamazaki says the budget grew by roughly thirty percent. It was shot entirely in Japan, and it's the first Japanese production filmed for IMAX.
