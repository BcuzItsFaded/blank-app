# Tenet, sketched — Treatment

**Style:** Urban Sketch · Pen & Wash (Lemo-Opuscar library).
**Topic:** the user's ~280-word spoken summary of Christopher Nolan's *Tenet* (2020), narrated in 15 lines (the same voice track as the risograph cut, ~125 s).
**Frame:** 1920 × 1080, 24 fps, ~127 s. A 2D camera over one large sketchbook page per place.
**Characters:** original stand-ins drawn as reportage sketch figures (faces left blank, per the style; no likeness of any actor), told apart by silhouette, costume, posture and wash colour.

## The idea

A spy's travel sketchbook. Every place in the story gets a page: Kyiv, a rail yard, a lab, Sator's coast, Oslo, Tallinn, a turnstile, Vietnam, Stalsk-12. The pen draws the world **forward**, in stroke order, as the story moves.

**Inversion is the pen running backward.** Anything inverted un-draws: its strokes retract from the tip toward where they began, and its wash shrinks back into the brush. An inverted bullet is a line that pulls itself back into the gun; an inverted car is drawn erasing itself as it drives.

**The one coloured thing is the orange trinket.** The pages are sepia ink with only a light, cool wash; the trinket is the only warm, saturated object, and it is the film's quiet protagonist (*The Red Balloon* grammar): we see it on the masked soldier in Kyiv, the camera loses it, and it comes back on Neil's bag at the end. Colour is held back to make that land.

## Three candidate structures

**A. The travel diary (chosen).** One page per place, turned by a hand or by a gust; a handwritten place and date in the corner of each; the camera travels each page on a spring and pushes in at story beats. The trinket appears on page 1 and on the last page. *Why:* the plot is a string of locations; a diary makes each one legible in seconds, and a page turn is the natural cut in this style.

**B. One street corner, many times.** A single Kyiv street drawn once, re-washed for each beat. Strong unity, but the story moves through nine places; a single corner would hide them.

**C. Forward page, backward page.** The first half drawn forward; at the turnstile the film plays the pages back in reverse. Clever, but the narration keeps moving forward, so playing pages backward would fight the voice for 60 s.

Chosen: **A**, with C's idea used locally — inverted things un-draw on the forward page.

## Colour logic

- **Ink:** one dark sepia, never changes.
- **Wash:** a restrained cool palette per page (blue-grey night for Kyiv, rail-yard slate, lab green-grey, coastal blue for Sator, wet asphalt for Tallinn, sunset ochre for Vietnam, dust for Stalsk-12).
- **Inverted things:** the same objects washed in a **cool rose**, the one shared "inverted" pigment, so pink-means-backward survives from the risograph cut without fighting the trinket.
- **The trinket:** cadmium orange, the warmest, most saturated mark in the film, with a paper halo.
- Paper white is the brightest value: reserved for faces, shirts, the trinket's halo and the sky.

## The cast (sketch figures)

- **The Protagonist:** tall, dark suit wash (indigo-grey), white shirt reserve, a narrow tie. Tactical kit and helmet in Kyiv and at Stalsk-12.
- **Neil:** loose linen jacket (pale ochre-grey), scarf, backpack; walks with his hands in his pockets.
- **Kat:** tall, belted trench (warm grey), hair up, long stride.
- **Sator:** heavy, broad, dark overcoat (near-black brown), hands behind his back.
- **The masked soldier:** helmet, gas mask, pack with the orange trinket.
- **Crowds:** carrot people (wash blob, two leg lines, a head dot) for the opera audience, the soldiers at Stalsk-12.

## Shot list (keyed to the narration)

1. **Opening — the blank page.** Only a handwritten date and "TENET" in the corner; the pen draws a clock; its hands move backward. Line 1.
2. **Kyiv, the opera house** (line 2): the pen draws the façade in stroke order; night wash arrives; tiny soldiers; push-in to the masked soldier and the trinket — the first warm colour. A bullet hole un-draws and the bullet line pulls back into the gun.
3. **The rail yard** (line 3): tracks to a vanishing point, a train's headlight; the seated, tied Protagonist; a pill drawn in the margin. Handwritten "loyalty test".
4. **The lab** (line 4): a bench, a bullet that rises into a hand as its stroke retracts; a handwritten diagram of entropy: an arrow forward in sepia, an arrow back in rose.
5. **Sator's coast** (line 5): a heavy figure on a terrace, a crate of gold bars washed in rose (inverted gold), a radio mast whose ink waves draw inward.
6. **The Algorithm** (line 6): nine parts sketched like a museum study page, numbered, then drawn together into a ring; the globe beside it, rose creeping across it.
7. **The team** (line 7): three figures walking toward us on a London-ish street; names handwritten above them.
8. **Oslo / Tallinn** (line 8): a page turn; an airliner drawn rolling into a freeport; then a highway with cars, one of them rose and un-drawing as it drives backward.
9. **Kat is hit** (line 9): an inverted Sator in rose; the line of the inverted bullet retracts from Kat back to his hand; Kat's wash drains to bare ink (implied, no gore).
10. **The turnstile** (line 10): a revolving door drawn as a diagram; two figures go in sepia, come out rose.
11. **Vietnam and Stalsk-12** (line 11): a sunset ochre bay with a yacht; a dashed ink signal across the page to a cutaway shaft at a Soviet blast site with the Algorithm at the bottom.
12. **The pincer** (line 12): carrot-people armies from both sides, the forward ones sepia, the inverted ones rose; a building collapsing on one side and re-drawing itself on the other.
13. **The end of it** (line 13): the Algorithm pulled up the shaft; on the yacht, Sator's wash drains to bare ink (implied); an inverted soldier steps in front of the Protagonist and his rose wash drains away.
14. **The trinket** (line 14): close-up page: Neil's bag, the orange trinket, the only saturated colour in the film; the camera holds while the pen draws it.
15. **The founder** (line 15): Neil walks off the page into the blank margin, turning rose as he goes; the Protagonist stays; handwritten "THE FOUNDER" in the margin; pull back to the whole sketchbook, which closes.

## Type and subtitles

All text is handwriting on the page: the place and date in each corner (Reenie Beanie), labels and titles in Caveat, subtitles written into the lower margin of the page as handwriting (never a box).

## Sound

Paper room tone, pen on cold-press paper (one burst per stroke), a wet brush for each wash bloom, a page turn between places, a reversed pen-scratch for every un-drawing. Score: a jazz waltz in 3/4 (walking bass, brushes, piano, a clarinet line) that drops to a bar of silence before Kat is hit, and resolves on the closing sketchbook. Mixed at −14 LUFS.
