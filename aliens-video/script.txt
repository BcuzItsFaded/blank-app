Ripley wakes up after fifty-seven years drifting in space. Earth moved on without her. And her daughter grew old and died while she slept.
Meanwhile, company man Carter Burke sends one colony family to check out a derelict ship. The father comes back with something on his face.
When the colony stops answering, the Colonial Marines suit up, cocky and loud. Ripley tags along, and she's the only one who isn't laughing.
Inside the empty colony, they find one survivor: a little girl named Newt, living in the air vents. Ripley swears she'll keep her safe.
The colonists' signals lead under the reactor. One stray bullet could blow it, so Gorman orders the ammo collected. Then the walls start moving.
The squad is shredded. Ripley drives in and drags the survivors out. Then the dropship coming to save them crashes in a ball of fire.
Trapped, they set up automated sentry guns that chew through wave after wave. But the motion tracker says something is already inside the room.
And the worst monster is human. Burke sent that family to the ship, and he wants a specimen smuggled home, inside Ripley and Newt.
Then the aliens pour through the ceiling. Marines fall. Hicks is burned by acid blood. And Newt is dragged down into the hive.
Ripley goes in alone and finds Newt, and the egg-laying Queen. One mother against another. Ripley burns the nest and runs.
Bishop flies them out seconds before the reactor explodes. But the Queen stowed away, and she tears Bishop in half.
So Ripley straps into a power loader, throws the Queen out the airlock, and this time, she doesn't lose her child.
