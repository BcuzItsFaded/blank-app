# La Planchada (impasto): treatment

Style: Impasto · Palette Knife. 9:16 vertical, 24 fps, 62.4 s (the last 3.4 s hold the ending card), English narration, plain-serif subtitles in a calm plane, no film grain.

**Structure chosen:** the same eight beats as the script, told as eight oil paintings. Alternatives considered: one long tracking shot down a painted corridor (too static for eight beats) and a portrait-in-layers built coarse to fine (no room for the legend's versions). Chosen because the medium has its own twist: **colour re-laid by the knife**. The haunted present is a cool, near-monochrome night; the flashback and the morning are re-laid in full colour by a wave of fresh strokes; at the heartbreak the colour is scraped back out, leaving only a red heart; the film ends with the one coloured thing, a small red heart.

**Logline:** a night nurse who loves her patients too much to leave them.
**Arc:** corridor at midnight → a perfectly pressed uniform under raking light → the betrayal (colour re-laid, then drained) → two versions of her death (paint-rain; a ghost pulled back by a ribbon of paint) → the night visit, with a push-in through silence and raking light → morning in colour, nobody knows her → the portrait with a black ribbon, a recognition, a flight → she tucks in a patient and the red heart rises; the light sinks until only the ridges glow, then black → the ending card laid with the knife.
**Native moves:** colour re-laid by the knife; the one coloured thing; raking light; paint-over sweeps between plates (with a scrape on the track); the light goes out.
**Benchmark (grammar only):** palette-knife street paintings (knife mosaic, wet reflections, warm lamps against cool shadow). No existing character or artwork is used.
**Sound:** acoustic and hand-made: solo violin and cello, harp arpeggios, piano, flute, glockenspiel-like plucks; knife scrapes on every sweep, wet dabs while the title is laid; hard silences: 21.0-21.5 s (the heart breaks) and 46.7-49.1 s (the push-in to the portrait), the second containing one harp harmonic.
**Titles:** laid in with the knife, stroke by stroke; subtitles in Cormorant Garamond italic.
