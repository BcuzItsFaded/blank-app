The Hunger Games — Complete 5-Book Storyboard

PlotTwistWith R&A | Easy summaries | Major plot twists | Endings explained

This covers all five published Hunger Games novels by Suzanne Collins.

Full spoilers ahead. I'll tell the stories in chronological order, from young President Snow to Katniss and the final rebellion.

The five books in story order

1. The Ballad of Songbirds and Snakes

The origin of President Snow

Easy summary: Young Coriolanus Snow mentors Lucy Gray Baird in the 10th Hunger Games. He falls in love with her and cheats to help her survive.

Plot twist: Snow betrays his friend Sejanus, who is executed. Later, Snow turns against Lucy Gray when she becomes a threat to his future.

Ending: Lucy Gray disappears into the wilderness. Her fate remains unknown. Snow returns to the Capitol, choosing power over love and becoming the ruthless leader we know. SparkNotes

2. Sunrise on the Reaping

Why Haymitch became a broken man

Easy summary: At the 50th Hunger Games, twice as many children are selected. Young Haymitch Abernathy tries to survive while helping a secret plan to sabotage the arena.

Plot twist: Haymitch wins after the last opponent's axe rebounds off a force field, but Snow punishes his defiance by killing his mother and brother and arranging the poisoning of his girlfriend, Lenore Dove.

Ending: Haymitch loses nearly everything and turns to alcohol. Years later, he helps Katniss and Peeta overthrow the Capitol, fulfilling Lenore Dove's dying wish. SparkNotes +1

3. The Hunger Games

The girl who starts a revolution

Easy summary: Katniss Everdeen volunteers to save her sister Prim and enters a deadly competition alongside Peeta Mellark.

Plot twist: The Capitol promises two winners, then suddenly changes the rules so only one can live.

Ending: Katniss and Peeta threaten to eat poisonous berries together. The Capitol allows both to win rather than have no victor. Their defiance becomes a symbol of rebellion.

4. Catching Fire

The Hunger Games were rigged against Katniss

Easy summary: Snow fears Katniss's growing popularity and forces her back into the arena for the 75th Hunger Games, along with other previous winners.

Plot twist: Some tributes, Haymitch, and Plutarch are secretly working with the rebellion. Their plan is to rescue Katniss, not simply win the Games.

Ending: Katniss fires an electrically charged arrow into the arena's force field, destroying it. Rebels rescue her, but Peeta is captured and District 12 is bombed.

5. Mockingjay

The shocking truth about the rebellion

Easy summary: Katniss becomes the Mockingjay, the public face of the revolution. The districts fight to overthrow President Snow.

Plot twist: Prim dies in a bombing, and Katniss realizes rebel leader President Coin may be just as dangerous as Snow. Coin proposes another Hunger Games using Capitol children.

Ending: Instead of executing Snow, Katniss kills Coin to stop the cycle of cruelty. Snow dies, the Hunger Games end, and years later Katniss and Peeta build a family together.

What the entire series really means

The biggest connection between all five books is that Snow uses fear, propaganda, and punishment to control Panem. Lucy Gray disappears while resisting his control. Haymitch loses his loved ones for defying him. Katniss finally brings down his government—and refuses to let Coin replace him with another version of the same system.

The final message is that winning a war isn't enough. The survivors must also choose not to repeat the cruelty of their enemies.
