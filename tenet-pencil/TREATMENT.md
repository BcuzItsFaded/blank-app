# Tenet, in a colored-pencil cutaway — treatment

Style: Colored-Pencil Science Illustration (`STYLE.md` is fixed). 1920×1080 (the style's native landscape frame, a cutaway needs the width), 24 fps, about 125 s.
English narration (male, calm, British storyteller voice), subtitles on a cream slip, bottom left.
Source script: the user's ~280-word summary of Tenet (2020), used as given.

## 1. Three candidate structures

A. **A plate that draws itself in reverse.** Open on the finished cutaway and un-draw it back to blank paper while the story plays backward. Strong inversion idea, but the twist would come first and the story would lose its build.
B. **One cutaway, sunk through.** The camera sinks through the ground line into a single soil section; each layer is a time (above ground: the present; deeper: the past and future). The story is a journey down the strata. Clear, but it puts the mechanism before the people.
C. **The blank half, drawn in as the story unfolds.** The film opens on a landscape whose underground is still blank paper. Each event is drawn in, layer by layer, and inverted things are drawn back out. The ending is the whole plate, complete.

**Chosen: C, with B's sink as the mechanism shot.** It gives the Tenet premise its own visual grammar: the drawing *is* time. The blank half is the unknown future; a stroke that un-draws is inversion. The twist then lands as the plate's last layer, drawn in on the trinket.

## 2. Logline and arc

One sentence: a cutaway of the ground that is drawn, undrawn and redrawn as an agent discovers that the war is being fought through time, and that the mission he ran was his own.

- **Setup (0:00–0:20):** the blank half; the opera siege drawn in; the backward bullet; the loyalty test; the orange trinket.
- **Turn (0:20–0:50):** inversion is shown as a stroke that runs backward; the vault below Sator's world fills with cobalt arrows from the future; the nine-ring Algorithm is drawn in one ring at a time; the team forms.
- **Escalation (0:50–1:25):** a freeport crash and a highway heist are drawn in cutaway; Kat is shot with an inverted bullet and the two men turn back through a turnstile; the magnifier in and out.
- **Climax and twist (1:25–2:00):** the temporal pincer at Stalsk-12; Kat stalls Sator and the Algorithm is pulled out; an inverted soldier saves the agent; the trinket on Neil's bag; the plate completes.

## 3. Benchmark

Learn: how a cutaway explains a mechanism; how a single pencil stroke can carry a motion (the backward grain); the magnifier as a change of scale; accents that always mean the same thing.
Don't learn: the story, the shots, the labels, the score (none of the demo's film is used).

## 4. Characters, places, copyright

- Every character is a **fictional stand-in**: a silhouette, a figure with a mark (the trinket), no likenesses, no costumes that copy the film. Names appear only in the narration and subtitles, as the user's script gives them.
- Places are generic: an opera house, a freeport, a highway, a vault, a Soviet-era blast site, a yacht. No recognisable real buildings, logos, aircraft liveries or film stills.
- The title is pencilled as the user's words, not the film's logo or typeface.

## 7. Cue map

- Tempo: 84 bpm in section 1 (a slow intro), 96 bpm in section 2, 116 bpm in section 3 (the heist), 72 bpm drone with pulse in section 4 (the climax), returning to 84 bpm for the plate.
- Instruments (acoustic, intimate, per `STYLE.md` §7): nylon guitar (section 1), bowed vibraphone (section 2), pizzicato bass and flute (section 3), harp and marimba (the turnstile), low cello drone (section 4), and a kalimba motif for the trinket, which plays only when the amber appears.
- Cuts land on the drawing's events: the horizon sweep on the downbeat; the magnifier change on a bar line; the inverted bullet's grain arrives on the beat that Kat's line starts.
- The narrator stays in 400 Hz–2.5 kHz: the music is kept light there.

## 9. Subtitles and titles

- Subtitles: English only, on a cream slip, bottom left, wiped on quickly. Keep the subject out of that corner.
- Titles: pencilled in the sky, stroke by stroke, with a hand-drawn underline. Section titles appear at most once per section.
- Labels: one cream paper tag per shot at most. Labels in section 4: "THE ALGORITHM" (once), "THE TRINKET" (once).

## 10. Accents (at most three, each one meaning)

- **Amber** = the orange trinket, and only the trinket. It appears in section 1 and in the ending.
- **Cobalt** = inverted things: arrows from the future, reversed cars, inverted soldiers, the reversed grain.
- **Red** = the Algorithm and the threat it carries: the nine rings' outlines and the forward front in the pincer.
