# La Mano Peluda — factual notes & sources

Episode 18 of **PLOT TWIST WITH A&R**. A ~60-second vertical (9:16) short in the
paper-cut lightbox style, English narration with the Spanish name and on-screen
Spanish cards. This file records what in the film is documented folklore versus
dramatization, so the narration stays honest.

## The legend
- **La Mano Peluda** ("the hairy hand") is a widely-told Mexican legend of a
  disembodied, hairy (sometimes severed) hand that emerges from under the bed at
  night and grabs a sleeper's hand, foot or ankle. It is a classic children's
  bedtime scare, told across Mexico and the Mexican-American Southwest.
- The film presents it as **legend and folklore**, not as a documented event.
  The narration explicitly says "no one agrees where it came from."

## The competing origin stories (all are folklore, not history)
- **The buried treasure / angry dead.** One common strand ties the hand to a man
  buried with a hidden fortune who claws up from the grave; the living who go
  looking for the gold are dragged down. Buried-treasure ghosts are a stock motif
  in Mexican oral tradition. Dramatized here (Scene 3, card **"ORO"** = gold).
- **The Inquisition.** Another strand says the hand belonged to a man tortured
  and mutilated during the **Spanish Inquisition** (active in New Spain / colonial
  Mexico via the Tribunal of the Holy Office, 1571–1820), who returns each night
  for revenge. The Inquisition in Mexico is historical; this *specific* attribution
  of the hand to it is legend. Dramatized here (Scene 4, card **"LA INQUISICIÓN"**).

## The "real mystery" framing
- **Sleep paralysis** is offered as a rational parallel: during sleep paralysis a
  person wakes unable to move and often feels a weight on the chest and a sense of
  a presence or a grip in the dark. This is a real, well-documented phenomenon and
  is a leading folkloric explanation for "night visitor" legends worldwide. Shown
  in Scene 7 (card **"¿PARÁLISIS DEL SUEÑO?"**).

## The surviving warning
- The practical rule the legend leaves behind — **do not let a hand or foot hang
  over the edge of the bed; keep your feet under the blanket** — is the genuine,
  commonly-repeated takeaway of the bedtime version. Scenes 6 and 8.

## Style
- Paper-cut lightbox: layered dark "cut-paper" silhouettes, warm backlight, cast
  shadows, film-grain. The recurring subject is the **hairy hand** rendered as an
  ink silhouette with bristles; the single warm light is a candle/lamp; one gold
  accent marks the buried treasure. No photographs, stock or AI imagery — every
  frame is drawn in code as a pure function of time.

_Nothing here asserts the legend is true. It is a folklore explainer._
