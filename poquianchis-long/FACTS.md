# Las Poquianchis — factual notes
Episode 25 of **PLOT TWIST WITH A&R** (paper-cut lightbox, ~59s, English). Condensed from the
"Mexico True Crime: 3 Cases Nobody Saw Coming" script and fact-checked against English and Spanish Wikipedia.

## Documented
- Four sisters, the **González Valenzuela** family (Delfina, María de Jesús, María Luisa and Carmen),
  ran brothels in **Guanajuato, Jalisco and Querétaro** from about 1950 until 1964 (the Spanish
  article says from 1945).
- They recruited young women through **deceptive help-wanted ads for housemaids**.
- In **1964**, **Catalina Ortega** escaped and reported to the judicial police in **León**.
- Police dug at the sisters' ranch near **San Francisco del Rincón** (**Rancho El Ángel**).
- The **surviving sisters were each sentenced to 40 years**. Carmen had died (sources differ on when).
- The case inspired Felipe Cazals's 1976 film *Las Poquianchis* and Jorge Ibargüengoitia's novel *Las muertas*.

## Disputed
- **The body count.** English sources report 80 women, 11 men and several fetuses ("at least 91").
  The Spanish article says only 8 victims are confirmed and that 91 was a sensationalist figure.
  The film says "reports claimed 91… historians still argue over the true number."

## Corrected from the source script
- "Loma del Ángel": the sources give Rancho El Ángel, so the film just says "the sisters' ranch".
- "The governor banned prostitution": not in the sources, so it was dropped.
- "Luisa got 27 years": both sources say 40 years each, so the film says "each sentenced to forty years".

## Long version (~2:20): added detail
- Origins: the sisters grew up in Jalisco, daughters of Isidro Torres, a policeman described as violent and
  alcoholic. After he shot a man, the family fled, changed its surname to González Valenzuela, and settled in
  San Francisco del Rincón, Guanajuato. (Birthplaces differ between the English and Spanish sources.)
- Delfina opened the first bar, and a back room proved more profitable than the bar itself. The operation spread
  across Jalisco, Guanajuato and Querétaro. Towns on the map are illustrative.
- Debt bondage kept women captive (Spanish source).
- Hermenegildo Zúñiga, "El Capitán Águila Negra", a former army captain, was Delfina's partner and the
  group's guard. The Spanish source alleges that local officials and police were complicit; the film says
  "according to later accounts".
- Arrests: Delfina and María de Jesús were arrested, and María Luisa fled at the last moment (Spanish source).
  The English source says the case broke through the arrest of a procuress, Josefina Gutiérrez. Both accounts exist.
- Fates: Carmen died of cancer (1949, before trial). Delfina died in prison in 1968 in an accident. María Luisa
  died in prison in 1984. María de Jesús was released and dropped out of sight (her death date is disputed).
- Legacy: Felipe Cazals's film *Las Poquianchis* (1976), Jorge Ibargüengoitia's novel *Las muertas* (1977),
  and a 2025 series. The newspaper headlines and the book and screen in the film are illustrative, not real covers.
- Victim count: 8 confirmed (Spanish source) vs at least 91 (English source) vs 150+ claims. All presented as disputed.
