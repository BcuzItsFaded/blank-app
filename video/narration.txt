One small doll. One serial killer. And decades of absolute madness. From the original Child's Play to killer A.I., Chucky has survived almost everything. Today we're breaking down every Chucky movie in order, and how this innocent-looking toy became one of horror's most unstoppable villains.
It all begins with Charles Lee Ray, a serial killer cornered by police inside a toy store. Before dying, he uses voodoo to transfer his soul into a Good Guy doll. That doll ends up with six-year-old Andy Barclay. But Chucky doesn't want to stay plastic forever. He needs Andy's body to become human again. After a terrifying confrontation, Andy and his mother finally stop him by destroying his heart.
Two years later, the toy company rebuilds Chucky, accidentally bringing him back. Andy is now living with a foster family, but Chucky still wants his body. The pursuit ends inside a massive toy factory, where Andy and Kyle finally destroy him with molten plastic and an exploding head. Or so everyone thinks.
Eight years later, Andy is a teenager at military school. And guess who's back? Chucky returns in a brand-new doll body. This time, he sets his sights on a younger boy named Tyler. Andy fights to stop him, leading to a terrifying amusement-park showdown where Chucky is shredded by a giant fan.
Then the franchise gets even crazier. Chucky's former girlfriend Tiffany brings him back to life, but he ends up trapping her inside a doll, too. Together they take a deadly road trip, mixing murder with the world's most dysfunctional romance. Even after both dolls are seemingly defeated, Tiffany gives birth to a mysterious new doll baby.
That baby grows up to be Glen and Glenda, Chucky and Tiffany's child, who just wants a peaceful family. But the parents have other plans. They head to Hollywood and become caught up in a wild story involving actress Jennifer Tilly. By the ending, Tiffany has taken over Jennifer's body, Glen and Glenda have human lives, and Chucky decides he'd rather remain a killer doll.
Years later, Chucky returns in a much darker story. A doll arrives at the home of Nica Pierce, and members of her family begin dying. The shocking truth? Charles Lee Ray knew Nica's mother long before becoming Chucky. His obsession with her helped cause the tragedy that left Nica unable to walk. Nica gets blamed for the murders, while Chucky escapes punishment.
Now Chucky has discovered a terrifying new trick. He can divide his soul between multiple bodies. Inside a psychiatric hospital, Nica finds herself surrounded by several living Chucky dolls. And the ending changes everything: Chucky successfully possesses Nica herself, escapes with Tiffany, and leaves Andy trapped inside the hospital.
The 2019 remake takes the story in a completely different direction. This Chucky isn't possessed by a serial killer. He's a smart Buddi doll with his safety programming disabled. His twisted attachment to Andy turns deadly, and he uses connected technology to terrorize an entire shopping mall. Even after Andy destroys him, another Buddi doll shows signs that the danger isn't over.
So what's the secret behind Chucky's survival? In the original films, voodoo keeps bringing the killer back through new bodies and new hosts. In the reboot, technology creates a completely different monster. And the original story doesn't end with the movies. It continues into the Chucky television series. After all these years, that tiny doll is still finding ways to haunt us.
