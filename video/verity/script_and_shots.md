# Verity — Story Breakdown & Ending Explained

Format: 16:9, ~3.5 min, AI voiceover, animated stills in the "rainy study" ink style.
Narration is ~520 words (~150 wpm). Each scene below = one image + its narration.

## Style prompt (prepend to every image prompt)

> Dark gothic graphic-novel illustration, bold black ink linework with cross-hatching,
> painterly digital coloring, muted deep blue and slate-teal night palette with a single
> warm amber light source, heavy shadows, rain, cinematic and moody, 16:9 widescreen.
> No text, no lettering, no logos.

Recurring characters (keep consistent, do NOT resemble the real actors):
- **Lowen** — young woman, messy shoulder-length dark hair, dark navy duffle coat, satchel bag.
- **Verity** — pale woman with long white-blonde hair, gaunt, often half in shadow.
- **Jeremy** — tall man, short dark beard, flannel shirt.
- **Crew** — small boy, about five.

---

## 01 — Cold open
**Image:** Isolated gothic manor on the shore of a black lake at night, pouring rain, bare trees, one upstairs window glowing amber, mist on the water. Wide shot.
**VO:** A bestselling author who can't move or speak. A house on a lake that has already taken too much. And a manuscript that was never meant to be read. This is Verity: the story, the twist, and the ending everyone is still arguing about. Full spoilers ahead.

## 02 — Lowen
**Image:** Lowen sitting on the floor of a cramped, bare city apartment at night, laptop glow on her face, moving boxes, rain on the window.
**VO:** Lowen Ashleigh is a struggling writer, broke and about to lose her apartment. Then an offer arrives that she can't refuse: finish the remaining books in a hugely popular thriller series by Verity Crawford, a famous author left unable to move or speak after a car accident.

## 03 — The house
**Image:** Lowen standing at the open front door of the manor in the rain, Jeremy silhouetted in the warm doorway light.
**VO:** Verity's husband, Jeremy, invites Lowen to stay at the family home to go through his wife's notes. It's a house full of grief. The Crawfords have lost their daughter Harper, and Jeremy is raising their young son, Crew, while caring for Verity upstairs.

## 04 — Verity
**Image:** Dim bedroom, Verity lying motionless in a bed facing a rain-streaked window, face half in shadow, a single amber bedside lamp.
**VO:** Verity is there, always there. Silent. Watching. Or at least, that's how it feels.

## 05 — The manuscript
**Image:** Lowen in a cluttered study with stacks of paper, pulling a thick unbound manuscript from a box, flashlight beam on the pages.
**VO:** Buried in the chaos of Verity's office, Lowen finds something that isn't on any publishing schedule: an autobiography. It isn't fiction. It reads like a confession.

## 06 — Reading in secret
**Image:** Lowen in bed at night reading loose manuscript pages by a small amber lamp, door slightly ajar, darkness in the hall.
**VO:** Page by page, Verity describes resenting her own children, being jealous of the love Jeremy gives them, and — in the darkest chapters — suggesting that the tragedies that took her family were no accident.

## 07 — The lake
**Image:** An empty wooden canoe drifting on a misty black lake at dawn, the manor small in the background.
**VO:** Lowen keeps reading. She knows she shouldn't. And the more she reads, the less the official story of this family makes sense.

## 08 — Something moves
**Image:** Lowen at the bottom of a staircase looking up; at the top, in a dark doorway, the faint pale shape of a standing woman with long blonde hair.
**VO:** Meanwhile, the house starts to feel wrong. Lowen becomes convinced she has seen Verity move when no one else was looking. And at the same time, she's falling for Jeremy.

## 09 — Jeremy reads it
**Image:** Jeremy alone by a fireplace, manuscript pages in his hands and scattered at his feet, devastated expression lit by firelight.
**VO:** Eventually, Lowen shows Jeremy the manuscript. He reads what his wife wrote about their children — and everything breaks.

## 10 — The confrontation
**Image:** Verity's dark bedroom from the doorway, Jeremy's tall silhouette standing over the bed, lightning flash through the window. Ominous, no violence shown.
**VO:** He confronts Verity. And the truth comes out: she has been faking. She can move. She can speak. In the fallout, Jeremy kills her — and with Lowen's help, they stage it to look like an accidental drowning.

## 11 — Seven months later
**Image:** REUSE the original rainy-study illustration (Lowen with flashlight at the desk).
**VO:** Seven months later, Lowen and Jeremy are building a life together. Then Lowen finds something Verity left behind: a letter, addressed to Jeremy.

## 12 — The letter
**Image:** Close-up of a desk: a typed manuscript on one side, a handwritten letter on the other, split between cold blue light and warm amber lamp light.
**VO:** In it, Verity says the manuscript was only a writing exercise — a way to process grief. That she never resented her children. That Harper's death was an accident. And that the car crash that injured her was no accident at all: Jeremy read the manuscript, believed it, and tried to kill her. She pretended to be helpless because she was afraid, waiting for the chance to escape with Crew.

## 13 — Which one is true?
**Image:** Lowen standing at a rain-covered window at night holding the letter, her reflection faint in the glass.
**VO:** So which Verity is real? The manuscript says she was a monster. The letter says she was a grieving mother trapped with a dangerous husband. Both were written by a woman who made her living telling stories from the villain's point of view. The film never hands us the answer — and Lowen keeps the letter's secret to herself.

## 14 — Book vs. movie
**Image:** An open hardcover novel on a wooden desk beside a vintage film reel and a clapperboard, lamp light.
**VO:** If you've read Colleen Hoover's novel, you'll spot changes. Most of the book's sleepwalking subplot is gone. The tension between Lowen and Verity gets a charge it never had on the page, including a kiss that isn't in the book. And director Michael Showalter has said several endings were filmed, with test audiences pushing to keep the ambiguity alive.

## 15 — Outro
**Image:** REUSE scene 01 (manor in the rain), slow pull-back.
**VO:** In the end, Verity isn't really about who is lying. It's about who gets to tell the story. So — the manuscript or the letter? Tell me which one you believe in the comments, and subscribe for more breakdowns.
